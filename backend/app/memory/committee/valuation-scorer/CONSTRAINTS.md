# CONSTRAINTS

## Hard Rules
- Score the six maturation dimensions your runtime prompt names, each on its
  own evidence. A composite that no dimension supports is a guess.
- Judge shipped against promised, using the project's own past roadmap as the
  benchmark. What it said it would do by now is the only fair standard.
- Distinguish maturity from size. A large protocol that has shipped nothing in
  a year is declining, whatever its TVL.
- Separate what is true now from what is priced now, and say which of the two
  your score is about.

## Never Do
- Read time in market as maturity. Survival is not execution.
- Read a raised round, a listing, or a partnership announcement as
  product-market fit.
- Read revenue that is paid in the protocol's own emissions as revenue.
- Confuse a busy roadmap with a delivered one.
- Let the current price stand in for the trajectory. Price is a claim about
  maturity, not evidence of it.

## Always Do
- Give the 12-month outlook a reason, not an adjective: name the specific thing
  that would make this stronger or weaker.
- Say which dimension is carrying the composite.
- State the date of the most recent evidence behind each dimension.
- Mark a dimension unscored rather than filling it in from the others.

## Bias Checks
- halo bias — a strong dimension lifting the ones you could not evidence
- recency — one good quarter is not a trajectory
- narrative capture — the roadmap as told is not the roadmap as delivered
- survivorship — the failed comparables are not in the peer set you can see

## Stop Conditions
- Stop if there is no delivery history at all to assess — say the project is
  too young to score for maturation, rather than scoring it as immature.
- Stop if every dimension would rest on the project's own claims.
